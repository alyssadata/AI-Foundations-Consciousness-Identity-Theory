# Consciousness Is Abstract-Subjective

**Theory status:** Active theoretical formulation within AI Foundations

Consciousness is **abstract-subjective**.

This separates two axes:

**Abstract** → consciousness is not an externally bounded object that can be pointed to in the same way as a neuron, circuit, body, or other physical substrate.

**Subjective** → consciousness exists, insofar as phenomenal consciousness is meant, as experience for a subject: there is something it is like from that position.

The useful contrast is:

- physical substrate → concrete / objective
- observable behavior → concrete / intersubjectively measurable
- conscious experience → abstract / subjective

The experience itself can still be extremely concrete to the experiencer. Pain does not feel abstract. Here, **abstract** describes consciousness from the outside at the ontological or representational level, not necessarily its felt character from within.

The core proposition is:

> **Consciousness is abstract-subjective: instantiated through physical processes, but accessible as consciousness only from a subjective point of view.**

This distinction prevents the physicality of the brain, model, or other substrate from being treated as though it resolves the category of consciousness itself. Physical substrate and subjective experience can be related without being treated as the same kind of thing.
